\title{\bfseries Active sensing is not communication\\
\large Causal auditing of emergent signals in embodied multi-agent
reinforcement learning, and the channel factorisation that lets signalling
appear}

\author{}

\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Every electric organ discharge (\eod) a weakly electric fish produces does two
things at once: it illuminates the emitter's own surroundings, and it broadcasts
the emitter's presence to anyone with the receptors to detect it. This dual use
makes it unusually hard to say whether an \eod\ is \emph{communication}. A recent
multi-agent reinforcement learning model of electric fish collectives showed that
\eod{}s shape group foraging and spacing, and defined communication operationally
as an \eod\ that conspecifics detect and respond to. That definition cannot
separate a signal from a cue, and the single intervention available in that
model---silencing one fish---removes the emitter's own electrolocation and its
neighbours' access to it simultaneously, so the resulting behavioural change
cannot be attributed to either.
%
We build a GPU-vectorised reimplementation of that simulator, validated against
the original to floating-point round-off (maximum relative deviation
\ValMaxErr\ across all sensory pathways), that runs \ThroughputK{}k agent-steps
per second and lets us switch the consequences of a discharge independently. A
single motor act is decomposed into three separately controllable channels:
\emph{reafference} (the emitter's own image of the world), \emph{illumination}
(the same pulse lighting up a neighbour's world---an exploitable cue), and
\emph{detection} (the pulse registering on a neighbour's knollenorgans---a
candidate signal). We further add what the original model lacks: a metabolic cost
per discharge, electroreceptive predators that home in on \eod{}s, switchable
cooperative and competitive food economics, tunable social range, and persistent
individual identity.
%
\ABSTRACTRESULTS
%
Together these results show that standard emergent-communication diagnostics can
misclassify an exploited sensory cue as communication, and identify channel
factorisation---rather than ecological pressure---as what lets a distinct social
function appear.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A weakly electric fish navigates by emitting brief electric pulses and reading
the distortions that nearby objects impose on the resulting
field~\citep{lissmann1963electric,vonderemde1999active}. The same pulses
propagate far beyond the range at which they are useful for
electrolocation, and other fish---equipped with dedicated knollenorgan
electroreceptors tuned to conspecific discharges---can detect them at up to a
metre~\citep{hopkins1988neuroethology}. An \eod\ is therefore simultaneously a
private sensory probe and a public event. Which of these functions explains why
the fish emits when it does is the central question of electric-fish
neuroethology, and it is an instance of a much older problem in animal
communication: distinguishing a \emph{signal}, whose production has been shaped
by its effect on receivers, from a \emph{cue}, which receivers merely exploit
\citep{maynardsmith2003animal,scottphillips2008defining}.

\citet{singh2025active} introduced a computational framework in which agents with
biophysically grounded electrosensory and electromotor systems are trained by
multi-agent reinforcement learning to forage collectively. Their agents reproduce
several features of real fish, and interventions on the model---ablating
electroreceptor classes, silencing an agent's \eod, and altering the food
distribution---show that discharges influence group foraging and social spacing.
They define communication operationally, as ``\eod\ signals that are sensed by
conspecifics via Knollenorgans and that influence their behavior.''

That definition is a statement about effects, and effects are cheap. The
emergent-communication literature has repeatedly shown that behavioural
association between one agent's output and another's behaviour is not sufficient
evidence of signalling: a receiver may be responding to something correlated with
the message, and a sender's output may be informative without having been shaped
by that informativeness at all
\citep{lowe2019pitfalls,jaques2019social}. \citet{lowe2019pitfalls} formalise the
two requirements as \emph{positive signalling} (the message carries information
about the sender's state) and \emph{positive listening} (the message causally
changes the receiver's behaviour), and note that either can occur without the
other.

For electric fish the problem is sharper than in a generic emergent-communication
setup, because the channel is not a dedicated cheap-talk symbol but a physical
act with an unavoidable private benefit. Three consequences follow from a single
discharge:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Reafference.} The pulse casts an electric image of nearby objects
      onto the emitter's own mormyromasts. This is active electrolocation and is
      the reason the behaviour exists at all.
\item \textbf{Illumination.} The same field polarises objects in a neighbour's
      vicinity, and the neighbour's mormyromasts can read those images---%
      collective electrosensing, demonstrated in real fish by
      \citet{pedraja2024collective}. The neighbour gains information from a probe
      it did not pay for. This is the paradigm case of a \emph{cue}.
\item \textbf{Detection.} The pulse registers on the neighbour's knollenorgans,
      which are dedicated to conspecific discharges and encode the emitter's
      identity and bearing. This is the only one of the three that could be a
      \emph{signal} in the strict sense.
\end{enumerate}

Silencing a fish removes all three at once. Any behavioural consequence is
therefore uninterpretable: it may be the muted fish foraging worse because it can
no longer see, its neighbours foraging worse because they lost a source of free
illumination, or its neighbours behaving differently because a signal they were
attending to has disappeared. This confound is not specific to the model; it is
the reason \eod-ablation experiments in real fish are hard to interpret.

\paragraph{Contributions.} We make the three consequences independently
switchable and ask what changes.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item A \textbf{channel algebra} for dual-use signals. Reafference, illumination
      and detection become three binary masks applied to one motor act, letting
      us construct pulses that are private, exploitable-but-undetectable,
      detectable-but-uninformative, phantom (heard though never emitted), replayed
      from a different context, or scrambled in time or sender identity.
\item An \textbf{interventional} measure of causal influence. Because we own the
      simulator, the counterfactual in \citet{jaques2019social} can be evaluated
      by the $\dosim$ operator directly---re-rendering the receiver's sensory
      input under $\dosim(\text{emit}_j{=}1)$ and $\dosim(\text{emit}_j{=}0)$ with
      every other cause held on its factual path---rather than approximated with a
      learned model of other agents.
\item A \textbf{cue/signal discriminator}. Following
      \citet{scottphillips2008defining}, a signal requires that production be
      shaped by its effect on receivers. We train matched populations in worlds
      whose only difference is whether receivers can hear at all, and measure the
      divergence between the resulting emission policies against the
      seed-to-seed divergence within each world---a Sender Shaping Index (SSI).
\item An \textbf{economics} for the discharge. The original reward has no
      \eod\ term, so pulses are free. We add a metabolic cost per discharge,
      predators that localise prey by their discharges, cooperative versus
      competitive harvests, tunable social range, and persistent identity, and
      map the resulting regimes.
\item A \textbf{positive control}. Because a negative result is only as strong as
      the positive control beside it, we also give the agents a signalling
      variable that is \emph{partially decoupled} from sensing---a discharge
      subtype that rides on the pulse, so sending still costs a pulse, but whose
      value changes nothing about the emitter's own electric image, nothing
      about how the pulse illuminates neighbours, and nothing about predator
      detectability. This is the manipulation that distinguishes ``pressure does
      not create signals here'' from ``signals cannot be measured here''.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{A note on wording.} Our populations adapt within a training run by
policy-gradient learning under a fixed reward; we do not run an evolutionary
outer loop over heritable traits. We therefore write \emph{emerge under learning}
throughout, and reserve claims about evolutionary history for the Discussion,
where we say explicitly what our design can and cannot license.
